Tempur-Pedic International, Inc. is an American manufacturer of mattresses, pillows and adjustable beds made with viscoelastic foam. A subsidiary of Somnigroup International (formerly Tempur Sealy International), it is headquartered on the Coldstream Research Campus in Lexington, Kentucky, and has manufacturing plants in Duffield, Virginia, and Albuquerque, New Mexico.[1]

The company was founded in 1992 to sell mattresses made by the Swedish foam maker Fagerdala World Foams in the United States. It went public in 2003 and acquired Sealy Corporation in March 2013. It became part of Somnigroup in February 2025, after its parent company acquired the retailer Mattress Firm.[2][5][7]

History

Founding

The first Tempur mattresses were made by Fagerdala World Foams, a Swedish technical foam company. The brand was brought to the United States in 1992, when Tempur-Pedic, Inc. was founded.[2] In 1999, nine Fagerdala distributors, including Tempur-Pedic, merged to form Tempur World Holding Company, which was reorganized in 2002 as Tempur-Pedic International Inc.[3] The company held its initial public offering in 2003.[4]

Acquisition of Sealy

In September 2012, Tempur-Pedic agreed to acquire Sealy Corporation for $2.20 per share, about $229 million, and to assume or repay Sealy’s debt, for a total transaction value of about $1.3 billion.[5] The Federal Trade Commission cleared the acquisition on March 8, 2013, and it closed on March 18, 2013.[6] The combined company was renamed Tempur Sealy International on May 22, 2013.[3]

Mattress Firm and Somnigroup

In May 2023, Tempur Sealy agreed to acquire Mattress Firm, the largest mattress specialty retailer in the United States. The Federal Trade Commission challenged the deal; after a federal court in Texas declined to block it, Tempur Sealy completed the acquisition on February 5, 2025, for about $5 billion and agreed to sell 176 retail locations to Mattress Warehouse.[7][8] On February 18, 2025, the parent company changed its name to Somnigroup International, with Tempur Sealy, Mattress Firm and the British retailer Dreams operating as separate business units.[7]

Technology and design

Tempur-Pedic products are built from Tempur material, stylized as TEMPUR, a viscoelastic memory foam that softens with body heat and conforms to the sleeper’s shape. Memory foam was originally developed in 1966 under a NASA contract; Fagerdala later adapted it for mattresses and pillows.[2] The company’s Breeze line adds cooling covers and layers intended to lower the temperature of the sleep surface.[10] In February 2024, the company announced a smart bed called the Tempur-ActiveBreeze. It has Sleeptracker-AI sleep monitoring and a built-in climate system that the company says provides a temperature range of up to 30 degrees.[13] It went on sale in April 2024, priced from $9,298 for a twin long.[14]

Products

Tempur-Pedic sells mattresses, pillows, mattress toppers and adjustable bases. Its mattress collections include foam and hybrid versions, such as the Tempur-Cloud, Tempur-Adapt, Tempur-ProAdapt and Tempur-LuxeAdapt, as well as the cooling-focused Breeze line.[9][10] It also sells pillows, including the Tempur-Neck, and the Ergo line of adjustable bases.[11][12]

Legal issues

In 2015, Tempur-Pedic’s German subsidiary was fined €15.5 million by Germany’s Federal Cartel Office for engaging in vertical price fixing between August 2005 and July 2011.[15][16]

Awards and recognition

In 2009, mattresses made of Tempur material earned the Good Housekeeping Seal,[17] and the company’s products received an Ease-of-Use Commendation from the Arthritis Foundation.[18]

In 2026, the Tempur-ActiveBreeze smart bed was a winner in the bed frames and bases category of the Good Housekeeping Bedding Awards,[19] and the Tempur-Neck pillow won in the wellness category of the HuffPost Shopping Awards.[20] Wired reviewed the ActiveBreeze smart bed the same year.[21]†

Editorial product guides have recognized Tempur-Pedic products, including:

  • Good Housekeeping: Best Overall luxury mattress, Tempur-ProAdapt (2026)[22]
  • Business Insider: Best memory foam cooling pillow, Tempur-Cloud Dual Cooling Pillow (2026)[12]
  • Apartment Therapy: Best foam mattress topper, Tempur-Adapt Topper (2026)[23]
  • Yahoo Health: Best pressure relief for side sleepers, Tempur-ProAdapt (2026)[9]
  • The Strategist: Best cooling foam mattress, Tempur-LuxeBreeze[24]†

Retail presence

Tempur-Pedic products are sold through independent furniture and mattress retailers and directly to consumers, online and in company-operated stores. They are also sold through Mattress Firm, which its parent company has owned since February 2025.[7][25]

Sealy (formerly the Sealy Corporation) is an American brand of mattresses marketed and sold by Somnigroup International. It takes its name from Sealy, Texas, where the company began in 1881.

Sealy grew through a licensing system that made it a national brand by the 1920s. Its largest licensee, the Ohio Mattress Company, acquired the company in the 1980s and took the Sealy name in 1990. Sealy was the largest U.S. maker of innerspring mattresses until 2011, and was acquired by Tempur-Pedic in 2013.[2][9]

History

Origins and licensing

In 1881, cotton gin builder Daniel Haynes of Sealy, Texas, began making cotton-filled mattresses for friends and neighbors. In 1889 he patented a machine that compressed cotton for his mattresses, and he later sold the rights to manufacturers in other markets.[1] In 1906, Earl Edwards bought the patents, took the Sealy name and built a national brand. Lacking money for factories, Sealy expanded by licensing: by 1920 it had 28 licensed plants.[1]

Ohio Mattress

The Great Depression cost Sealy most of its licensees, and the company consolidated with those that survived.[1] Its largest licensee, the Cleveland-based Ohio Mattress Company, run by Ernest M. Wuliger, sold into territories reserved for other licensees and filed an antitrust suit against Sealy in 1971.[2]

In 1983, Ohio Mattress bought the premium mattress brand Stearns & Foster.[2]

The antitrust case was decided in 1986 in Ohio Mattress’s favor, with a $77 million award. Unable to pay, Sealy and all but one of its other licensees sold to Ohio Mattress, which became the world’s largest bedding manufacturer by sales.[2]

Buyouts and public listing

The merchant bank Gibbons, Green, van Amerongen took the company private in an April 1989 leveraged buyout. Its bridge lender, First Boston, was left holding the loan when the junk-bond market dried up, and the deal became known as “burning bed.”[3] Ohio Mattress adopted the Sealy name in 1990.[2] Bain Capital and Sealy executives acquired the company in 1997, and in 1998 Sealy moved its headquarters from Cleveland to North Carolina. Kohlberg Kravis Roberts acquired it in 2004 in a deal valued at $1.5 billion,[4] and Sealy announced an initial public offering in 2005.[5]

Tempur Sealy and Somnigroup

In September 2012, Tempur-Pedic agreed to acquire Sealy for $2.20 per share and to assume its debt, a transaction valued at about $1.3 billion. As of 2012, Sealy operated 25 U.S. plants and held 17.8 percent of U.S. mattress sales.[6][7] The acquisition closed in March 2013, and the combined company was named Tempur Sealy International.[8] In February 2025, Tempur Sealy acquired the retailer Mattress Firm and changed its name to Somnigroup International.[10]

Technology and design

Sealy introduced the Posturepedic line in 1950, developed with input from orthopedic surgeons.[15]† Posturepedic mattresses use innerspring coil units with added support under the center third of the body, where the most weight rests.[11]† Sealy’s hybrid models pair those coil units with foam comfort layers.[13]

Products

Sealy makes innerspring, hybrid and foam mattresses and adjustable bases. Its main line is Sealy Posturepedic, a range of coil-based mattresses built around the Posturepedic support design and sold in innerspring and hybrid versions at several price levels.[11][13] It also sells Ease adjustable power bases[12] and Cocoon by Sealy, an all-foam mattress sold online and shipped compressed in a box.[14]† Stearns & Foster, which Sealy has owned since 1983, is sold as a separate brand.[2]

Awards and recognition

In 2026, Good Housekeeping named the Sealy Posturepedic Spring its best mattress under $1,000[11] and the Ease Power Base its best adjustable bed for seniors.[12] The same year, the Posturepedic collection won a BrandSpark Best New Product Award, which is based on a consumer survey,[16] and Sealy ranked second among crib mattress brands in Newsweek’s America’s Most Trusted Brands survey.[17]

Editorial reviews and product guides have recognized Sealy products, including:

  • PureWow: PureWow100 score of 95, Posturepedic Elite Brenham (2026)[18]
  • Forbes Vetted: Review of the Posturepedic Elite Brenham (2026)[19]†
  • The Strategist: Best less-expensive cooling hybrid, Posturepedic Elite Hybrid[20]†
  • Yahoo Health: Best affordable mattress for side sleepers, Posturepedic Hybrid (2026)[13]†

Retail and international markets

Sealy mattresses are sold online and through independent furniture and mattress retailers. They are also sold through Mattress Firm, which its parent company has owned since February 2025.[10]

Licensees operate in Australia, Argentina, the Bahamas, Colombia, Israel, Jamaica, Japan, New Zealand, South Africa, Thailand and the United Kingdom. Sealy began exporting to South Korea in 1995 and manufacturing in Mexico in 1996 and South Africa in 2001. In 2011 it opened its first plant in China, a 100,000-square-foot factory outside Shanghai run as a joint venture with licensee Sealy Australia.[21]

Stearns & Foster is an American mattress brand founded in 1846 in Cincinnati, Ohio, by George S. Stearns and Seth C. Foster.[1] The company began as a maker of cotton wadding and later operated a large factory in Lockland, Ohio, for about a century.[2] It has been owned by Sealy since 1983 and is part of Somnigroup International.[3]

History

Cincinnati and Lockland

George Sullivan Stearns, a Massachusetts businessman, and Seth Cutter Foster, a Kentucky cotton-goods merchant, founded the company in Cincinnati in 1846 to make cotton wadding and other cotton goods, including upholstery for carriages. It later began making mattresses, which it sold to hotels.[1] In the late 19th century the company moved to Lockland, along the Miami and Erie Canal, where its plant grew to more than a million square feet.[5]† At its peak in the 1970s, the Lockland plant employed more than 1,200 people.[4]

Ohio Mattress and Sealy

In 1983, the Ohio Mattress Company, then the largest Sealy licensee, purchased Stearns & Foster as a premium brand.[3] In 1989, after Ohio Mattress was sold in a leveraged buyout, its former chief executive Ernest M. Wuliger tried to buy Stearns & Foster for $87 million.[3] Ohio Mattress took the Sealy name in 1990. Sealy closed the Lockland mattress operations in 1993, citing the plant’s limited capacity and age; the textile business at the site continued separately.[2] A 2004 fire damaged the vacant plant,[4] which was later demolished.[5]†

Tempur Sealy and Somnigroup

In 2009, the brand redesigned its mattress line.[1] Tempur-Pedic acquired Sealy, including Stearns & Foster, in March 2013,[6] and the parent company was renamed Somnigroup International in 2025.[7] Stearns & Foster was named among the brands at issue when the Federal Trade Commission challenged Tempur Sealy’s acquisition of Mattress Firm.[7]

Technology and design

Stearns & Foster mattresses are built on an innerspring coil unit with foam comfort layers above it. The company describes its coil system, called IntelliCoil, as a coil within a coil that firms as weight increases.[1]

Products

Stearns & Foster sells innerspring and hybrid mattresses, organized into collections and offered in firmness levels from soft to firm, including pillow-top versions.[8][9]† The brand is positioned above Sealy in price.[10]†

Awards and recognition

In 2026, Forbes Vetted named the Stearns & Foster Reserve its best luxury mattress in its Best Product Awards.[11] Wirecutter, the product review site of The New York Times, has recommended the Estate in its guide to innerspring mattresses.[8]

Retail presence

Stearns & Foster mattresses are sold through furniture and mattress retailers, including Mattress Firm, which the parent company has owned since 2025.[7]
